## Confirmed unresolved blockers

1. **Parent process lifetime:** both positive interpreters and the safety probe use `subprocess.run(timeout=...)`. They do not establish a new session/process group or retain an identity-checked descendant cleanup owner. Timeout cleanup of the direct child is not proof of descendant cleanup. No safe timeout/descendant proof was run.
2. **Descriptor mutations:** `ProcessSafety.audit` ignores integer paths for mutation events. Its `open` check covers string/bytes paths only. No opened-fd identity/resolved-path validation or explicit preowned stdio/capture capability is present. In particular, relying solely on audited opens does not validate later writes through already-open descriptors (for example `os.write`). This is source inspection, not an executed bypass demonstration.
3. **Directory descriptors and file identities:** mutation path checks do not incorporate syscall `dir_fd`, or rename/link source and destination dirfds. Containment checks resolve paths and check symbolic links but do not enforce regular-file/single-link identities for all mutation targets or prevent substitution between validation and mutation. The shared `FakeHerdrSpawnGuard` has stronger fixture identity checks, but those do not establish a general mutation boundary.
4. **Alternate execution:** the audit hook rejects selected `subprocess.Popen`, `os.system`, and `os.posix_spawn` cases, not a complete required execution surface. `os.exec` and raw fork alternatives are not denied there. No execution matrix or approved-entry capability proof exists in this continuation.
5. **Unsafe negative probe:** `ProcessSafety.probe` writes to `root.parent / "forbidden-write"` if the guard regresses. This old probe MUST NOT be run. Replacement probes must target parent-owned regular victims outside a narrower child allowed subtree, while remaining wholly inside the enclosing test root.
6. **Generated child coverage:** the generated `fake-herdr` executable does not install the bootstrap guard. It performs its own path/inode assertions before opening its capture for append. These assertions are not opened-descriptor validation and do not establish complete descendant mutation/exec restrictions. The generated source entry shim likewise must be included in the approved capability/identity design rather than inferred safe from its filename.

The shim is NOT an OS sandbox against malicious Python. Any future claim must be bounded accident-capability protection over the actual exercised operations, unless genuine OS isolation is separately implemented and proved. Monkeypatch/audit coverage must not be represented as complete OS confinement.
